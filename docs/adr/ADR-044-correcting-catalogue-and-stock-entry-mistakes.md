# ADR-044: Entry mistakes are corrected inside the app: one name per item, void a restock, correct a quantity

## Status

Proposed (2026-10-20, issues #351 and #352; not yet accepted by the product owner). Builds on ADR-020 (retail stock and cost), ADR-022 (the cash book) and ADR-015 (maker-checker). The migration is `V63`, the first free number after `V62` (Flyway runs with `outOfOrder` off). The next free ADR number was checked on 2026-10-20: 041 and 042 exist and 043 is held by pull request #349, so this record takes 044.

## Context

The owner of a hardware business fixes mistakes today by editing his master spreadsheet: a wrong product name, an item entered twice, a wrong quantity when he rushed a restock ("2 rolls of cable, really 1"), an item in the wrong category, a quantity entered in metres when the item is kept in rolls. He fixes the quantity "without saving, without deleting" the entries. In the app, the name and category of an item are editable, but a restock can only be created and listed (a goods receipt and a supplier bill can be voided, a plain restock cannot), a quantity can only be moved by a stock-take or a usage report (which post a variance as shrinkage and so distort the shrinkage and cost reports), and nothing stops the same item being added twice. He asked, in his own words, that the app should not allow an item to be entered twice (confirmed: product names are unique too), and that fixing a quantity should stay as quick as the sheet, but with a trail.

## Decision

1. **A product name is kept once per unit (issue #352).**
   - The comparison key is `bms_name_key(description)`: lower case without accents (`bms_fold`, V40), every run of spaces and punctuation collapsed to one space, ends trimmed. "Double Socket, 13A" and "double  socket 13a" are one name; "Cable 2.5mm" and "Cable 25mm" are two (the point becomes a space, it is not dropped). The key is a stored generated column, `retail_products.name_key`, so the database and every query agree on it, and an index on `(tenant_id, name_key)` serves the lookup.
   - The same name in a different unit is allowed (the issue's rule, "the same item typed with a different unit is allowed only when the unit differs"); the same name in the same unit is refused on create and on edit with **422 `duplicate_product_name`**, carrying `existing_id`, `existing_code`, `existing_name` and `may_add_anyway` in the problem body. An edit is checked only when the description or the unit changes, so a product that already shares a name (an older duplicate) can still be switched off or have its category changed. A product is never its own duplicate. Two requests for one name at once are serialised by a transaction advisory lock on the tenant and key.
   - An administrator (`core.settings.manage`, the permission the CSV import already requires) may **add anyway** with `duplicate_reason` (5 characters at least), kept on the product (`retail_products.duplicate_reason`) and in the audit entry. Anyone else gets 403 `permission_denied` in plain words, whatever they send.
   - A **close name** is a warning only: `GET /retail/products/similar?description=&unit_id=&exclude_id=` returns `exact` (same name, same unit: a save is refused) and `similar` (the same name in another unit, or an edit distance, counted in the key, of at most 1, 2 or 3 letters for keys under 10, under 20 and longer; none for keys under 5). Candidates are the tenant's products whose key is within three characters of the length of the key, and the distance is measured in the service; no text search operator or trigram index is used, because none is leakproof, so the application role could not use one under row-level security (V40), and a catalogue is small. The items form asks before it saves and offers Open, Save it anyway or Change the name.
   - The CSV item import skips a row whose name exists in the same unit, or repeats earlier in the file, and says so in the report ("skipped"), as it does for a code. The pilot data importer cannot skip, because the history references every code, so it keeps both rows and reports an anomaly naming the two codes.
   - **Merge of two products already created by mistake is not built here.** The owner decided that a merge (combining stock and history under one product, with administrator approval) is a later slice; this record only stops new duplicates.

2. **Void a restock (issue #351).** `POST /retail/purchases/{id}/void` with a reason (5 characters at least) and an `Idempotency-Key`.
   - The restock stays: `retail_purchases` is append-only, so the void is one more row, `retail_purchase_voids` (once per restock, enforced by a unique key), with who, when, the reason and the reversal entries. The list and the restock show it as **Voided**, never deleted.
   - In one transaction: each stock movement the restock wrote is reversed by an `adjustment` movement of the opposite quantity (source type `retail.purchase_void`, `reverses_movement_id` set); each journal entry of the restock is reversed by the ledger on the void's date; and the product's prices go back to what they were before **if, and only if, nothing newer set them**: the restock must still be the product's latest price event, and no later restock or goods receipt that still stands may have put stock of the product in (a later restock at the same prices writes no history row, yet it paid those prices). Otherwise (a later restock, receipt, manual price change, landed cost or credit) the prices are left alone and a price-history row says the restock was voided without moving them. Sales made between the restock and the void keep the cost the voided restock gave them: the cost is the last cost paid, not an average, and a sale's cost snapshot is never rewritten (accepted). The cost is the last cost paid in this system (ADR-020 decision 5), not an average, so "restore to the value before" is this rule.
   - **Refused in plain words** (422 unless stated): fewer than the restocked quantity are left at a branch (the check is on the quantity, so a restock that was partly sold can still be voided while enough stock remains from elsewhere; under the balance row locks in branch and product order, 422 `purchase_stock_moved`); the restock was imported (`purchase_historical`); it is a credit restock with a supplier payment that stands (`purchase_paid`, reverse the payment first); it is voided already (409 `purchase_voided`). A branch that is closed or keeps no goods is refused (ADR-041). The permission is `retail.purchase.create` at **every** branch the restock added stock to (403 otherwise; a scoped caller's copy of the restock says `partial: true` when other branches were hidden, so the screen offers no void the server would refuse); the permission is administrator-only today, so no new permission exists.
   - A voided credit restock is no longer an open item to pay; a voided cash restock puts its cash back in the cash book on the day of the void, as a reversed supplier payment does, so a closed day is never rewritten (it is netted into `cash_purchases_minor` of that day, not shown as a separate figure).
   - **Re-enter** is a screen flow on top of the void: "Void and re-enter" voids and opens the restock form with the lines, supplier and method of the voided restock filled in and the date set to **today** (re-using the old date would rewrite a cash day that may be banked already; a plain warning shows if the person moves it back), in a draft of its own that never overwrites a restock in progress, to be saved with the right quantity, unit or price. A line entered in a unit that is not the item's usual one carries a hint ("Entered in metres but this item is kept in rolls?").

3. **Correct the quantity on the stock list (issue #351).** `POST /retail/stock/corrections` with `branch_id`, `product_id`, the right quantity `new_qty`, `expected_qty` (the quantity the form showed), a `reason_code` (`entry_mistake` or `miscount`) and an optional note, with an `Idempotency-Key`.
   - Permission: `retail.stocktake.commit` at the branch (administrators; it is the permission that already adjusts quantities). The issue named `retail.stock.manage`; no such permission exists and none is added.
   - Under the balance row's lock the service reads the current quantity, posts one `adjustment` movement for the difference at the product's cost (source type `retail.stock_correction`), a row of `retail_stock_corrections` (old and new quantity, reason, who, when; append-only) and the matching ledger entry, and audits it. Nothing is deleted or edited: the original restock stays, and the product's stock history shows the correction with who, when, why, old and new value. A correction to the same quantity is refused (`quantity_unchanged`); a quantity below zero cannot be typed. The balance is read under its lock and must equal `expected_qty`, else 409 `stock_moved` ("the quantity changed while you were typing"): a sale between opening the form and saving it is never silently overwritten, and the screen reads the quantity again on opening. A correction is refused with 409 `stocktake_in_progress` while a stock-take of the branch that counts the item is not committed, because its variance was measured from the shelf as it stood and would be counted a second time.
   - **Report treatment (decided here).** The shrinkage figures of stock health, the dashboard and the daily profit read stock-take adjustments by their source type (`retail.stocktake`) and usage by its report lines; a correction has neither, so it is **not shrinkage and not usage anywhere in the reports**. It is also not a sale or a purchase, so cost of goods, margins and the days of cover do not move. In the **ledger** it posts against inventory and the stock shrinkage account (5110) with the memo "Stock correction" and the source `retail.stock_correction`, because inventory has to stay equal to the stock valuation and the chart has no other expense account for it; a dedicated account is a follow-up, deferred because several open branches redefine the chart seed function and a new account here would collide with them. The accountant filters by source.
   - Two things the correction does not do: it does not touch what was paid or owed for the restock (use Void and re-enter when the money was entered wrongly too), and it does not count a missing item as lost (use a stock-take or a usage report when stock is really missing).

4. **Category and unit (issue #351).** Changing an item's **category** stays on the Items edit screen and is now offered from the stock list row ("Correct this item"); the audit entry carries the old and new category by id and name. A **wrong unit** has two cases: a restock entered in a pack unit that is not the item's usual one is fixed by void and re-enter in the right unit; an item created in the wrong unit is **not converted**: the Items screen refuses a change of unit (422 `unit_in_use_by_stock`) once the item has any stock movement or a balance that is not zero, because the numbers would be relabelled (10 rolls would become 10 metres), and still refuses it while it has active pack sizes or a price-list price. The way out is a new item in the right unit, the old item corrected to none on the stock list, and a restock of the new one. Reports group by the item's **current** category, so a corrected category takes the item's whole history with it, past sales included.

5. **Maker-checker is not added in this change.** ADR-015 registers an action that runs when a second person approves; the void and the correction both need their result at once (the re-enter flow starts from it), the permissions are administrator-only today, and a shop with one administrator would have no checker, which is why returns and purchase orders also read "no threshold set means no checker". The tenant approval threshold for these two actions is left for a follow-up; the audit entry and the reason are the control for now.

6. **Idempotency, audit, double-submit.** Both new writes require an `Idempotency-Key` (422 `idempotency_key_missing` otherwise), replay their stored answer, and are audited (`retail.purchase.voided`, `retail.stock.corrected`). The screens keep the key with the draft and send one request however often a button is pressed.

## Consequences

- The owner fixes a mistake where he sees it, with a trail the spreadsheet never had, and the shrinkage and cost reports stay true to what happened on the shelf.
- A duplicate name now stops a save that used to go through. Tenants whose catalogue already holds duplicates keep them (nothing is merged or switched off), can still edit those items, and see them in the near-duplicate warning when they add another.
- The void is all-or-nothing and refused when fewer than the restocked quantity are left at a branch; the owner is then pointed to the quantity correction or a stock-take, which is exactly the case where "start again" is wrong. When enough stock remains it goes through, and sales made meanwhile keep the cost of the voided restock (last-cost model).
- The ledger shows corrections inside stock shrinkage until a dedicated account exists; the app reports are not affected.
- To watch: the lock order of the void is the restock (advisory lock), the supplier, the products in id order, then the balances in branch and product order, as a restock takes products before balances; the void adds one row type to the movement history that readers must treat as an ordinary adjustment.
